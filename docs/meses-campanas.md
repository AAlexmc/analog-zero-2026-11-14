Comparison points (own choice): Jezero crater for Mars, lunar south pole (89.45° S, 137.15° W) for the Moon. Only the parameters of each mission that are compared with that point are used (the Earth-only ones compare a site with itself). The Moon or Mars value stays at its yearly value. **Summary.** 11 campaign-and-world pairs whose match changes with the month (10 more use terrain parameters only and are the same all year). NASA's month is the site's best month in 0 of 11, and within 5 points of it (own threshold) in 5; median rank of NASA's month: 10 of 12.

**Polar sites (beyond 60° of latitude).** 7 pairs; rank of NASA's month: 11, 12, 12, 10, 10, 12, 11 of 12; they lose 1.9 to 13.1 points against their best month. NASA went in local summer; by temperature and light these sites are most like the Moon or Mars in local winter.

Limits: this does not say NASA chose badly. The month of a campaign depends on access, daylight for the crews, budget and safety, none of which is measured here; and several of these tests (drills, radar, rover driving) do not need the coldest month. A 0.5° cell is not the campaign spot. A campaign that lasted the whole year is left out. The match is our own metric, not a probability.
